## 2026-09-10 — session 1: pin budget and bank map

**D1. Data sources.** Pin attributes come from prjtrellis-db `ECP5/LFE5U-{85,45,25}F/iodb.json` (vendored in `pinmap/db/`, CC0), parsed programmatically; nothing was assigned from memory. Rules come from Lattice FPGA-TN-02032 (sysIO), FPGA-TN-02035 (high-speed I/O + DDR3 pinout), FPGA-TN-02039 (sysCONFIG), TN1263 (sysCLOCK) and FPGA-DS-02012 3.4 (datasheet). Reference designs read: ULX3S (`ulx3s_v20.lpf`, KiCad symbol), ButterStick and OrangeCrab (litex-boards platform files). Dedicated (non-PIO) ball numbers — TCK T5, TMS U5, TDI R5, TDO V4, PROGRAMN W3, INITN V3, DONE Y3, CCLK U3, CFG0 U4, CFG1 T4, CFG2 R4 — come from the ULX3S KiCad symbol and must be re-checked against the Lattice caBGA381 pinout CSV before the schematic is released.

**D2. Drop-in 45F/25F.** Only balls bonded on all three parts carry signals (197 of 205). The 8 others (A15, C9, D10, E10, K16, K17, T16, M5) stay unconnected; the generator refuses to assign them.

**D3. Package orientation.** Package rotated 180° from the datasheet top view: A1 at the front-right, row A toward the front (HDMI/USB-host/audio) edge. Bank 1 front-left top, bank 0 front-right top, banks 2/3 left side (front/back halves), banks 7/6 right side (front/back halves), bank 8 + JTAG/config in the back-right corner (so the FT2232H, flash and PC-uplink USB-C sit at the back). Chosen so the TMDS pairs (bank 7) run straight to a front-right HDMI connector and the DDR3 chip sits on the left with no connector behind it.

**D4. Bank voltages.** Banks 0, 1, 6, 7, 8 = 3.3 V; banks 2, 3 = 1.35 V (DDR3L only). No 2.5 V bank: nothing needs true LVDS drivers, and 3.3 V PMODs were valued more (fit-report item 7 lists the 2.5 V option).

**D5. DDR3L on banks 2+3 (left side), split DQ bank / address bank.** Data (DQ, DM, DQS) entirely in bank 3 using the two complete DQS groups RDQS89 (lane 0: DQS T19/R18) and RDQS53 (lane 1: DQS N16/M17); address/command/control/CK in bank 2. This mirrors ButterStick (same 85F caBGA381, proven with LiteDRAM) with the banks used the same way, keeps every DQ within its DQS group, keeps the bank-3 VREF1 (P16) free as required, and gives the layout one bank facing the DRAM's DQ balls and one facing its address balls. Lattice's "start DQS groups from the middle of the edge" SSN advice was weighed against ButterStick's proven grouping; the proven grouping won. CK is J19/K19 (PCLKT2_1/C A/B pair). DQ bits within a byte lane and address bits may be swapped at layout time **only by editing `assignment.toml`** (LiteDRAM does not support address swapping in software).

**D6. HDMI on bank 7 A/B pairs, not on the top side.** Lattice: top banks have no edge clocks, 1x gearing only, single-ended interfaces only. ULX3S puts GPDI on top-side pins and overclocks; we use right-side pairs (C4/B4 clock, A2/B1, C1/D1, F4/E3 data) so ODDRX2 + ECLK is available. IO_TYPE LVCMOS33D DRIVE=4 SLEWRATE=FAST as on ULX3S. 1080p60 is still above the datasheet rating (fit-report item 1); the -8 speed grade is recommended.

**D7. Clocks.** 27 MHz oscillator on F2 (PCLKT7_0), Si5351A CLK0 on G3 (PCLKT7_1), I2S MCLK on H5 (GR_PCLK7_0). All are primary-clock pads and reach every PLL through the primary clock network (Lattice sysCLOCK guide); the nearest PLL is the upper-left one (absent on the 25F, where LL/LR remain reachable). Dedicated GPLL input pairs kept free: U16/T17 (LRC_GPLL0, bank 3, 1.35 V spare), P3/P4 (LLC_GPLL0, on the 2x20 header), A4/A5 (ULC_GPLL0, PMOD A), A6/B6 (ULC_GPLL1, SD card-detects), A19/B20 (URC_GPLL1, FMC reset / button).

**D8. PMODs.** All 32 PMOD pins are on pad pairs: PMOD A/B in bank 7 (2 A/B + 6 C/D pairs), PMOD C/D in bank 6 (8 A/B pairs = the true-LVDS-driver pairs). Pair-to-PMOD-pin mapping: (1,7) (2,8) (3,9) (4,10) per PMOD spec 1.3.1.

**D9. STM32H743 FMC on bank 1 (front-left top).** 16 data + 8 address + NOE/NWE/NE + IRQ + NRST = 29, async, LVCMOS33 fast slew. Bank 1 was chosen because the FMC is a mid-speed single-ended bus (fine on a top bank) and the STM32/USB PHY/hub naturally sit at the front-left near the USB host ports. Three user buttons take the last three bank-1 balls.

**D10. Bank 0 (front-right top): microSD x2 (7 each incl. card-detect), ESP32-C6 SDIO link (8), I2C (2).** All low-speed single-ended, so the top bank is appropriate. SD sockets are expected at the front-right/right-front; ESP32 module placement is free (8 slow lines).

**D11. Bank 6 (right-back): PMOD C/D, reset button (K1), 2x20 header (15 pins = 7 pad pairs incl. PCLKT6_0/C and the LLC_GPLL0 pair + H1).** *(Updated by D18: K1 joins the header, 16 pins in bank 6.)* The header gets clock-capable and PLL-capable pins on purpose.

**D12. Bank 8: Master-SPI quad flash on W2/V2/Y2/W1 + CSSPIN R2 (SCK = CCLK U3), CFG[2:0] = 010; 8 active-low LEDs on the remaining dual-purpose pins** (R1 T1 U1 V1 T2 U2 R3 T3). *(Superseded by D17: LEDs now via a 74HC595.)* LPF: `MASTER_SPI_PORT=ENABLE`, slave ports disabled. Flash pull-ups per Lattice: 4.7k on CSSPIN, 10k on MOSI/MISO/IO2/IO3, 1k on MCLK.

**D13. Additions beyond spec:** sd1_cd, sd2_cd, hdmi_hpd (3 balls). Not on FPGA: HDMI DDC/CEC (STM32 suggested), FMC NBL/NWAIT/CLK, ESP32 BOOT strap. See fit-report item 6 — to be confirmed by the owner.

## 2026-09-10 — session 2: fit-report decisions applied

**D16. Speed grade -8 for all three parts.** Device is now LFE5U-85F-8BG381C with LFE5U-45F-8BG381C / LFE5U-25F-8BG381C as drop-in alternates (`assignment.toml [package]`, LPF header, `check_nextpnr.py --speed 8`). Datasheet ratings that matter: GDDRX2 TX 800 Mb/s, DDR3L 800 Mb/s, ECLK 400 MHz.

**D17. HDMI target = 720p60, 1080p30 in spec, 1080p60 best-effort.** TMDS assignment unchanged (bank 7, A/B pairs, LVCMOS33D DRIVE=4 FAST). Schematic: series resistors + optional AC coupling per the ULX3S GPDI circuit. HPD removed from the FPGA (E2 → header pin ext[21]); HPD, DDC and CEC belong to the STM32H7.

**D18. LEDs via 74HC595 on bank 8:** SRCLK = R3 (DOUT/CSON), SER = T2 (SN/CSN), RCLK = U2 (CS1N). Rationale for this pin choice: these three pins have config-time meaning (weak pull-ups; WRITEN-style strap behaviour) and only ever see a CMOS input load, so nothing on the board can disturb configuration; the "clean" tristated D[7:4] pins go to the header instead. RTL must refresh the register right after configuration (or tie 595 /OE to inverted DONE). Freed for the header: V1, U1, T1, R1 (D4..D7) and T3 (WRITEN, keep a pull-up, never pull low from the header).

**D19. Buttons (3) and reset button read by the STM32H7**, exposed to the FPGA via an FMC register and the existing `fmc_irq`. K1, D12, E12, B20 go to the header. The STM32 drives `fmc_nrst` for the FPGA fabric reset.

**D20. microSD card-detect removed** (A6/B6 → header, an ULC_GPLL1 pad pair). Card presence by polling CMD0/CMD8.

**D21. Header = 27 pins:** 16 in bank 6 (8 pad pairs), 3 in bank 1, 2 in bank 0, 1 in bank 7, 5 in bank 8. The four 85F/45F-only balls stay unconnected. Naming `ext[0..26]`; partners listed in `bank-summary.md`.

**D22. 1.35 V spares:** pseudo-power pads N19 (VCCIO), T20 (GND), M18 (VCCIO) as SSTL135_II outputs, tied on the PCB; reserved-not-connected second-rank pins J20/K20 (CK1±), E19 (CS1#), U17 (CKE1), ODT1 = shared or T17 later; everything else stays spare / test point.

**D23. Ownership confirmations:** DDC + CEC on the STM32H7 (5 V-tolerant pins or level shifter for DDC); FMC is 16-bit-only, 8-bit address, no NBL/NWAIT/CLK; ESP32-C6 BOOT strap on an STM32 GPIO beside its programming UART, EN stays FPGA-driven and is requested through an FMC register; DDR3 single rank this revision. ## 2026-09-10 — session 3: block diagram and power tree

**D28. PC-uplink USB-C carries only the FT2232H.** Channel A = FPGA JTAG (MPSSE, openFPGALoader/OpenOCD), channel B = UART to the STM32 with DTR/RTS wired to BOOT0/NRST so the STM32 ROM bootloader can be used without a debugger. The STM32's OTG_FS device port is not connected to the uplink (would need a second hub); STM32 firmware update runs over the UART, ESP32 firmware over the STM32 UART pass-through, FPGA flash over JTAG. Option kept: a 2-port hub (USB2422) on the uplink if a native STM32 USB device is wanted later.

**D29. Power input = USB-C PD sink (12 V preferred, 9 V, then 5 V) into a 4 A 5V_SYS buck.** Worst case ≈ 25 W at the input exceeds a 5 V/3 A source; on a 5 V-only contract the STM32 limits the USB host port switches. Candidate PD controller CH224K (alternative STUSB4500); STM32 reads VIN via ADC.

**D30. Rail architecture and sequencing.** Rails: 5V_SYS, 3V3_STBY (always on, STM32 + RTC + PD controller), 3V3 (3 A buck), 1V35 (2 A buck), VTT/VREF 0.675 V (TPS51200 from 1V35, feeds DRAM VREF and FPGA VREF1_3 at P16), 2V5 (LDO from 3V3 for VCCAUX), 1V1 (3 A buck, FPGA core), per-port 5 V USB switches, HDMI 5 V limiter. Sequence MAIN_EN → 3V3 (+2V5) → PG → 1V35 (+VTT/VREF) → PG → 1V1, satisfying "VCCIO before or with VCC/VCCAUX" and the ECP5 POR on VCC/VCCAUX/VCCIO8; PG-chained so the hardware sequence needs no firmware timing. All rail current estimates are in `power-tree.md` and must be re-checked with the Lattice Power Calculator once RTL exists.

**D31. Standby domain owned by the STM32H743:** 3V3_STBY powers the STM32 (Standby mode, WKUP from the soft power button), RV-3028 and the PD controller; MAIN_EN switches everything else. Soft power-off is a clean de-assert of MAIN_EN; the FPGA configuration is lost on every power cycle (reloaded from flash).

**D32. Hub port allocation:** USB2514B downstream 1–2 → USB-A (stacked dual on the left edge since D47), 3 → USB-C host (left edge) (host only, 5 V/1.5 A capable when VIN ≥ 9 V), 4 → internal 4-pin header (spare).

**D33. Clock tree:** 27 MHz XO → FPGA (system/HDMI/DDR3 PLLs); Si5351A CLK0 → FPGA, CLK1 = MCLK → codec + FPGA, CLK2 spare; STM32 HSE 25 MHz; USB3320 and USB2514B each with their own 24 MHz crystal; RTC internal 32.768 kHz.

## 2026-09-10 — session 5: schematic

**D41. Two hardware simplifications found while drawing the power sheet.** (a) The 5V_SYS buck/bypass switchover is done in hardware from VIN, not by firmware: the TPS54560B EN divider enables the buck only above ≈7 V and the LM66100 CE divider disables the bypass above ≈6.5 V, so a PD supply stepping from 5 V to 12 V can never put 12 V on 5V_SYS through the bypass (the firmware-controlled variant in D38 had that hazard because the STM32 is not yet running when the CH224K negotiates). The two STM32 "buck EN / bypass EN" pins are removed. (b) USB port power switches are enabled by the USB2514B's own PRTPWRx outputs with the TPS2553 FAULT lines returned to the hub's OCS_Nx inputs (standard per-port power switching, CFG_SEL=00); the STM32 no longer needs six EN/FAULT pins and instead gets HUB_RST_N (PG15). At a 5 V-only contract the port budget is enforced by the fixed 1.1 A ILIM per port plus the hub's over-current handling; the STM32 can still hold the hub in reset. `stm32-pin-plan.md` updated.

**D57. Staged routing, escape geometry and the DDR3 pin-swap experiment.** The user asked why DDR3 was not routed first and whether other standard practices were being skipped, so the flow was rebuilt around priority staging (D51/D54 had routed everything at once). What is now in place and why:

*Practices adopted.* (1) Priority staging: stage 1 routes only the DDR3 group and the differential pairs (TMDS, USB, DQS, CK) with wider spacing (0.15 mm gap, 3W would be 0.2), everything else is exported with its copper as an obstacle but without pins (`ROUTE_ONLY`); stage 2 routes the rest around them. Freerouting 1.9 cannot be told to fix wires — one `fix`-typed wire makes it treat every wire as fixed and route only copper-less nets — so staging works by stripping pins, not by fixing (export_dsn.py). (2) Fiducials (3 per side), test points on every rail, thermal reliefs on through-hole ground pads (gen_pcb.py). (3) A parts-free routing corridor between the DRAM's east edge and the FPGA's west edge (`CORRIDORS` in gen_pcb.py): the first staged run had 22 decoupling capacitors and their vias there and every DDR3 route looped around them. (4) The DRAM escapes all leave towards the FPGA (`EXIT_SIDE`): the nearest-edge rule had sent half of them west, round the part. (5) Every DDR3/TMDS ball gets a deterministic escape (priority within each ring, ring-1 power dog-bones inward, via sites one step beyond the edge rows, corner channels) and plane_vias.py keeps a 1.5 mm / 0.25 mm zone in front of every stub end free — a 1V35 plane via 0.125 mm in front of a stub end had made DDR3_DQ5 unroutable even alone. (6) The outer-layer escape comb is staggered (ring-1 stubs one pitch longer than ring-2 channel stubs) so the router can turn at a stub end at 0.2 mm clearance; this took the stage-1 leftovers from 40 to 5 of 92 nets. (7) Sessions are merged per net and never replace a net's copper with something less connected (merge_ses_nets.py): a sub-stage had stripped the escape stubs of nets the router ripped up and failed to re-route. (8) GND stitching next to every high-speed via and on an 8 mm grid (stitch_vias.py), length tuning by meander insertion (ddr3_tune.py), a DDR3/pair report (route_report.py).

*The pin-swap experiment (kept in the repo, not used).* DDR3 allows the DQ bits (and DM) of a byte lane to be permuted freely at the FPGA within the lane's DQS group, the two lanes to be exchanged, and the address/command lines to be permuted within their bank; `ddr_swap.py` computes the crossing-free assignment from the escape stub order (address group 140 crossings -> 0, lanes 26/29 -> 0) and rewrites `pinmap/assignment.toml`. With it, `ddr_corridor.py` lays straight, chamfered, 0.2 mm-spaced lines across the corridor. Result: the corridor router placed 16–19 of the 50 DRAM–FPGA nets (shared-row stub ends on different layers and via placement conflicts blocked the rest), and Freerouting, on the swapped board, stalled at 27–38 open nets in every variant tried (whole board, per-group sub-stages, 0.125/0.15/0.20 mm, staggered stub ends), against 5 on the unswapped board with identical geometry — the parallel, non-crossing bus is apparently the worst case for its maze/rip-up search. The swap was reverted (the two escape tweaks made for it too); the tools stay for an interactive or dedicated-router pass later.

*Result of stage 1.* Stage 1 on the router-friendly board: 88 of the 92 priority nets routed at 0.15 mm spacing (open: DDR3_RAS_N and the hub's DN2/DN3/DN4 pairs at the QFN's 0.5 mm pins, all left for the interactive pass). The maze router's DDR3 lengths are the weak point: lane 0 19–45 mm around a 25 mm strobe, lane 1 20–37 mm, address/command 17–48 mm against a 36.5 mm clock; the meander tuner (ddr3_tune.py, bumps sized to the shortfall) brings the pairs and the few nets with a straight run within tolerance and reports the rest: 19 nets have no straight segment long enough for a meander and 10 (DQ2, DQ6, DQ9, DQ11, DQ13, DQ15, A3, BA0, CS_N, ODT) are longer than their reference and must be rerouted shorter by hand.
